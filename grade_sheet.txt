QUESTION: Which parties must be included in the Letter of Agreement that covers Runway Safety Area activities at a towered airport?

REQUIRED FACTS: Technical Operations

ANSWER:
Based on the documents retrieved, here is what I can tell you:

According to **AC 150/5210-20A**, Section 3.2.1, at airports with an operating Air Traffic Control Tower (ATCT):

> *"To accomplish this task, the FAA at each towered airport is creating with the airport operator a letter of agreement outlining standard operating procedures to include delineating movement and non-movement areas."*

So the two parties that must be included in the Letter of Agreement are:

1. **The FAA (Air Traffic Control Tower)** – at each towered airport
2. **The Airport Operator**

The documents note that when activities need to take place in the RSA, they should occur either between aircraft operations or when a runway is closed via NOTAM (Section 3.1), and that during construction, the airport operator can follow the ground vehicle practices contained in **AC 150/5370-2, Operational Safety on Airports During Construction** (Section 3.2.1).

The source for this is **AC 150/5210-20A**, Section 3.2.1.

QUESTION: At an airport without an operating control tower, where should vehicle operators announce their intentions?

REQUIRED FACTS: CTAF

ANSWER:
Based on the documents I've found, here's the answer:

At an airport without an operating control tower, vehicle operators on the movement areas (runways and taxiways) must monitor and announce their intentions on the **Common Traffic Advisory Frequency (CTAF)**.

According to **AC 150/5210-20A, Appendix B, Section 4.1**: Any vehicle driving on the movement areas must be capable of monitoring and transmitting on the **CTAF**.

Additionally, per **Section 3.2.2** of the same document: At airports without an operating ATCT, vehicles, fixed-based operators, or others can use two-way radio control, and everyone should pay attention to frequencies used by aircraft and announce their positions and intentions on the appropriate frequency.

==================== q-02 ====================
QUESTION: How can vehicles that are not marked and lighted operate on the movement and safety areas of an airport?

REQUIRED FACTS: escort

ANSWER:
Based on the FAA Advisory Circular AC 150/5210-20A, here is what I found regarding vehicles that are not marked and lighted operating on movement and safety areas:

The key mechanism is **escorting**. According to Appendix B, Section 4.1 of AC 150/5210-20A:

> **"A vehicle that is equipped with a radio and a driver who is movement area qualified may escort vehicles or anyone towing an aircraft without radios; these vehicles must stay under the control of the escort at all times."** [AC-150-5210-20A, Appendix B, Section 4.1]

So for vehicles that are not marked and lighted (and/or lack radios), they can operate on the movement and safety areas by being escorted by a properly equipped vehicle that:

1. **Has a two-way radio** capable of contacting the ATCT (air traffic control tower) or monitoring the CTAF (Common Traffic Advisory Frequency).
2. **Has a driver who is movement area qualified** (has received the required training and authorization from the airport operator).
3. **Maintains continuous control** over the escorted vehicle(s) at all times.

Additionally, the general requirements state:
- Vehicles may not enter the movement area or cross runways unless the operator has received required training and authorization from the airport operator [AC-150-5210-20A, Appendix B, Section y].
- Any vehicle driving on movement areas must have contact with the ATCT or be capable of monitoring and transmitting on the CTAF [AC-150-5210-20A, Appendix B, Section 4.1].
- Permission must be requested and clearance given prior to driving on a movement area [AC-150-5210-20A, Appendix B, Section 4.1].

Note: If a movement area is closed for construction (via NOTAM), vehicles may traverse that area without ATCT contact as an alternate scenario [AC-150-5210-20A, Appendix B, Section 4.1].

The FAA publishes a list of the companies offering courses of instruction in line service training 
as well as supervisory training that are acceptable to the Administrator.  Because changes may 
occur more frequently than the schedule for updating the AC, the FAA will review this list on a 
quarterly basis and post an updated version online as an Addendum to this AC.  
The airport operator is responsible for consulting the current listing of acceptable training 
courses.  Likewise, the companies listed in this Addendum are responsible for notifying the FAA 
of any changes to their training syllabus and the availability of the course(s) offered or to contact 
information.
